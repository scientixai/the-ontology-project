Risk-based monitoring (RBQM)

A KRI breach at a single site triggers a chain of investigation, escalation, unscheduled monitoring, root-cause analysis, and CAPA. TOP makes that chain a graph — one query assembles it from signal to resolution.

Operator screen — KRI dashboard & signal lifecycle — GLP1-TRIAL-27

The impossible screen. “Show me the current risk posture across all sites — which KRIs are breaching, which site triggered an unscheduled monitoring visit, and trace that signal from the raw EDC data through the risk model to the CAPA being worked today.” Today: the risk management plan lives in a Word doc in the eTMF, KRI scores are computed monthly in Excel from EDC exports, monitoring visit records live in CTMS, deviation logs live in a separate module, and CAPA actions live in a quality management system. Connecting them for one signal takes a day of manual work. With TOP: one screen, one query, in under a second.

Screen assembled inUnder 1 second · one query

Why this view is impossible today. Risk-based monitoring requires correlating data across systems that have no native connection. The risk management plan lives in a Word document in the eTMF. KRI thresholds are defined in that document but computed monthly by a statistician who exports query-aging data from the EDC, SDV completion from the monitoring system, and deviation counts from the deviation register — and pastes them into Excel. A KRI breach triggers an email chain, not a graph query. By the time the email reaches the CRA, context has been lost.

What TOP does differently. Every element of the RBQM chain is a connected record the system understands — the CtQ factor, each KRI definition and reading, the risk assessment, the mitigation plan, the monitoring visit, and the CAPA. The KRI threshold lives on the KRI definition itself, not as a number typed into a Word document. A breach creates a risk assessment linked back to the readings that triggered it; the monitoring visit is linked to that assessment; the CAPA is linked to the visit’s findings. Because those links are real, the whole chain — signal to resolution — assembles in a single query.

Regulatory traceability. ICH E6(R3) Section 5.18 requires that the sponsor’s monitoring plan be documented and that monitoring findings be traceable to corrective actions. In TOP, that traceability is structural: KRI reading → risk assessment → monitoring visit → CAPA. A regulatory inspector can follow the same chain the clinical team uses.

Two queries power the entire screen. The first assembles the site KRI matrix; the second traces the Denver signal lifecycle from breach to CAPA.

### Query 1 — KRI matrix (all sites, latest reading per indicator) ###
GET /ngsi-ld/v1/entities?
  type=cr:KRIReading
  &q=prov:wasAssociatedWith==urn:ngsi-ld:top-cr:Study:GLP1-TRIAL-27
  &join=inline
  &joinLevel=2
  &options=temporalValues
  &lastN=1

Accept: application/ld+json

### Query 2 — Denver signal lifecycle (breach → CAPA chain) ###
GET /ngsi-ld/v1/entities/urn:ngsi-ld:top-cr:RiskAssessment:GLP1-TRIAL-27:denver:RA-2026-0088?
  join=inline
  &joinLevel=4

#   RiskAssessment

#         → SDVFinding ×8
