External lab data transfer (DTA / DTS)

How outside vendor data — safety labs, ECG, imaging, eCOA — becomes clean, mapped study data, set up once and reused.

Most of a modern trial’s data is generated by outside vendors, not on your CRFs, and getting it into the study database is governed by a Data Transfer Agreement (DTA) and its Data Transfer Specification (DTS). Today both are Word/Excel documents, rebuilt per vendor, per data type, per study, and re-touched at every amendment — then hand-mapped to SDTM by a programmer. This page shows how that transfer becomes a machine-readable contract that renders SDTM automatically, and stops for a human only where it genuinely should.

The screen a manual DTA can’t give you. A central-lab file lands with a 10-analyte safety panel for one subject at Visit 3. Eight results map themselves to the standard concept, standardise their units, resolve the vendor’s visit label, and pass straight through to SDTM. Two stop — one because the vendor’s term matched only weakly, one because it has no standard code at all — and wait for a single human sign-off. Confirm those two once and they never stop again: the match joins a shared library, and the next study on this vendor auto-resolves them. No programmer wrote a mapping.

How the transfer runs

Match once, reuse forever. A crosswalk here is a matching table the whole industry shares and keeps adding to. The vendor calls it SODIUM; the standard concept is Sodium, identified by its LOINC code (2951-2). Match it once and the match is shared — the next sponsor on the same vendor gets it for free. This replaces the per-study “encounter mapping” table that today is re-typed and stored inside every DTA.

Units and visits on a common footing. mEq/L standardises to mmol/L (UCUM), the conversion done the same way every time; the vendor’s visit label V3 D29 resolves to your protocol’s Visit 3.

SDTM is rendered, not authored. Once a result is matched to its concept, unit and visit, the SDTM LB row, the Dataset-JSON, the Define-XML and the human-readable DTA document all come out as views. Nobody hand-writes SDTM. The two flagged rows simply wait for one sign-off before they render.

What the lab sends — a FHIR R4 Observation (the shape Labcorp, Quest and the health-data networks already speak). Two of the ten entries shown: one that auto-resolves, one that stops for attestation.

What’s checked before data lands

These run automatically at ingestion — the “data validation & quality” layer a manual DTA leaves to a late reconciliation pass:

SituationWhat happens
A test not matched to a standard concept, with no attestationBlocked — it can’t be rendered to SDTM
A unit that isn’t a known standard unitBlocked — no safe conversion
A file format or transfer method outside the agreed listBlocked
The person who signed the transfer isn’t authorisedBlocked
A match the system is only somewhat sure of (below threshold)Flagged for one confirmation — then it joins the shared library
A study-specific item with no standard codeFlagged for a clinician attestation once at the catalogue
Why grade it? A genuinely unusable transfer is stopped; a “probably fine, please confirm” is flagged, not stopped. A manual process can only do this with people and time; here it is built into the contract and runs on every file — and the flagged tail shrinks every study as the shared library grows.

Two audiences, one mechanism

For the study team / site manager

You stop rebuilding the same lab mapping every study. Vendor test names, units and visit labels are matched to your protocol once; the next study on the same vendor is already matched. A wrong unit or an off visit label surfaces when the file arrives as a flag to confirm — not months later as a reconciliation query.

The CDISC 360i vision

This is the CDISC 360i Digital DTA goal made concrete: the DTS becomes a machine-readable contract generated from the USDM protocol, not prose. SDTM is rendered from it — never a stored target a human fills in. The validation & quality work usually cut from an MVP (conformance, unit checks, visit reconciliation) is built in and runs at ingestion — the CORE / Dataset-JSON conformance the 360i roadmap calls for.
